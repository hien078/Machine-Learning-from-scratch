Must Have · Area 1

Linear Algebra

1.1

Why #1

All data in AI = tensors (generalized matrices). Every neural network layer = a linear transformation + a nonlinearity. Understanding linear algebra = understanding the backbone of deep learning.

1.2

Core Topics

TopicWhy You Need It
Vectors, vector spacesEmbeddings, feature spaces
Dot product, outer productSimilarity, attention
Matrices, matmul, transposeForward pass = matmul chain
Rank, kernel, image, subspacesLow-rank, LoRA, bottleneck
Determinant, traceJacobian, normalizing flow
Eigenvalues, eigenvectorsPCA, spectral methods, Hessian stability
Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)PCA, low-rank approx, recommender systems
Norms: L1, L2, L∞, Frobenius, nuclear, spectralRegularization, weight decay
Symmetric, PSD/PD matricesCovariance, Hessian, kernels
Projection, orthogonalityGram-Schmidt, QR, orthogonal init
Kronecker product, Hadamard productEfficient computation
LU, QR, Cholesky decompositionSolving linear systems, sampling
1.3

AI Applications

  • Forward pass: $y = \sigma(Wx + b)$ — just matmul + bias + nonlinearity.
  • Attention: $\text{softmax}(QK^\top / \sqrt{d})\,V$ — all linear operations.
  • PCA: diagonalize the covariance matrix = SVD.
  • LoRA: $\Delta W \approx BA$ with $\text{rank}(BA) \ll \text{rank}(W)$ — low-rank factorization.
  • Batch Normalization: whitening = projection onto a subspace.
  • Embeddings: points in $\mathbb{R}^d$.
  • GNN: Graph Laplacian $L = D - A$ is a linear algebra matrix.
1.4

Recommended Resources

  • Foundation: Gilbert Strang — "Introduction to Linear Algebra" (accessible, MIT 18.06 lectures).
  • Rigorous: Sheldon Axler — "Linear Algebra Done Right" (determinant-free approach).
  • For ML: Chapter 2 of "Mathematics for Machine Learning" (Deisenroth et al.) — free PDF.
  • Advanced reference: Trefethen & Bau — "Numerical Linear Algebra".
1.5

Self-Check

  • Compute SVD by hand for a 2×2 matrix.
  • Explain why attention divides by $\sqrt{d}$ (hint: variance of dot product).
  • Prove that the covariance matrix is always PSD.
  • Implement PCA in NumPy without np.linalg.svd (use power iteration).
  • Why does LoRA work with rank $r \ll d$?
1.6

Common Pitfalls

Warning
  • Confusing rank with dimension.
  • Forgetting that $A^\top A \neq A A^\top$.
  • Assuming eigenvalues are always real — only true for symmetric/Hermitian matrices.
  • Confusing "orthogonal" with "orthonormal".
Must Have · Area 2

Multivariable Calculus

2.1

Why #2

All training = optimizing a loss function over millions/billions of parameters. Backpropagation = chain rule applied on a computation graph. Without calculus, you can't understand why models learn.

2.2

Core Topics

TopicWhy You Need It
Partial derivativesEvery gradient component
Gradient $\nabla f$Direction of steepest ascent of loss
Jacobian $J$Vector→vector derivative, backprop through layers
Hessian $H$Curvature, second-order methods, sharpness
Chain rule (single & multi-variable)Backpropagation
Matrix/vector derivativesComputing $\partial L / \partial W$
Taylor expansionLinearization, second-order approximation
Multi-variable integrationMarginalization in probability
Implicit function theoremImplicit layers, equilibrium models
Leibniz ruleDifferentiating under the integral, reparameterization
2.3

AI Applications

  • Backpropagation: generalized chain rule for function composition.
  • Reverse-mode autodiff: why PyTorch/JAX are fast — efficient Jacobian-vector products.
  • Reparameterization trick (VAE): $z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$ enables gradient flow through sampling.
  • SAM (Sharpness-Aware Minimization): uses Hessian/curvature.
  • Natural gradient: $F^{-1}\nabla L$ where $F$ is the Fisher information matrix.
  • Score matching: $\nabla_x \log p(x)$ — gradient of log-density.
2.4

Self-Check

  • Compute $\partial(x^\top A x)/\partial x$ by hand.
  • Derive the gradient of cross-entropy loss through softmax.
  • Implement backprop from scratch for a 2-layer MLP in NumPy.
  • Explain the reparameterization trick: why is it necessary for VAE?
  • Draw a computation graph and compute reverse-mode autodiff by hand.
2.5

Common Pitfalls

Warning
  • Thinking the gradient is a scalar — it's always a vector.
  • Forgetting the transpose when differentiating with respect to matrices.
  • Assuming the Hessian is always computable — for large models, it can't be stored.
  • Confusing JVP (forward-mode) with VJP (reverse-mode).
Must Have · Area 3

Probability & Statistics

3.1

Why #3

ML = learning from data = learning distributions. Every loss has a probabilistic interpretation. Generative models = learning $p(x)$. Bayesian DL, uncertainty estimation, A/B testing — all need this.

3.2

Core Topics

TopicWhy You Need It
Discrete & continuous random variablesEvery stochastic model
Distributions: Bernoulli, Categorical, Gaussian, Poisson, Exponential, Beta, DirichletLikelihood, prior
Expectation, variance, covarianceLoss, regularization
Joint, marginal, conditional distributionsBayes, graphical models
Bayes' theoremMAP, Bayesian inference
MLE, MAP, EMParameter learning
Central Limit Theorem (CLT)Why Gaussian is ubiquitous
Law of Large NumbersSGD convergence
Sampling: rejection, importance, MCMC, GibbsBayesian inference, diffusion
Variational inference, ELBOVAE, variational methods
Hypothesis testing, p-value, confidence intervalsModel evaluation, A/B
Concentration inequalities (Markov, Chebyshev, Hoeffding)Generalization bounds
Exponential familyUnified framework
3.3

AI Applications

  • Cross-entropy loss: $-\mathbb{E}[\log p(y|x)]$ = negative log-likelihood.
  • VAE: maximize ELBO $= \log p(x) - D_{KL}(q(z|x) \| p(z))$.
  • Diffusion model: learns the score $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ at multiple noise levels.
  • Bayesian Neural Network: prior on weights, posterior via VI or MCMC.
  • Uncertainty estimation: epistemic vs. aleatoric uncertainty.
  • Active learning: select sample with highest entropy.
  • GAN: ultimately optimizes a form of divergence.
  • RL: policy = distribution over actions.
3.4

Self-Check

  • Derive cross-entropy = negative log-likelihood of Bernoulli/Categorical.
  • Derive the ELBO of VAE in two ways: (a) from $D_{KL}(q\|p_{\text{posterior}}) \geq 0$, (b) from Jensen's inequality.
  • Explain why log-likelihood is usually better than likelihood (numerical stability).
  • For a Gaussian model, compute MLE of $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ by hand.
  • Implement Gibbs sampling for a simple model.
  • Distinguish: epistemic uncertainty vs. aleatoric uncertainty.
3.5

Common Pitfalls

Warning
  • Confusing $p(A|B)$ with $p(B|A)$ — base rate fallacy.
  • Forgetting the Jacobian when changing variables.
  • Confusing posterior with likelihood.
  • Assuming MLE and MAP always give the same result (only when prior is uniform).
  • Forgetting that KL is asymmetric: $D_{KL}(p\|q) \neq D_{KL}(q\|p)$.
Must Have · Area 4

Optimization

4.1

Why #4

Training = optimization problem. Understanding optimizers = understanding why Adam usually outperforms SGD, why learning rate matters, why models get stuck at saddle points.

4.2

Core Topics

TopicWhy You Need It
Convex vs. non-convexDL is non-convex; understand the difference
Gradient descent, SGDBasic optimizer
Momentum, NesterovAcceleration, escaping local minima
Adaptive methods: AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam, AdamWModern optimizers
Learning rate schedulesWarmup, cosine decay — vital for LLMs
Second-order: Newton, quasi-Newton (L-BFGS)When applicable
Constrained optimization, Lagrange, KKTSVM, RLHF
DualityDeep understanding of SVM, convex problems
Stochastic optimization, variance reductionSGD analysis
Convergence analysis (rate, bounds)Reading optimization papers
Sharpness, flatness, generalizationGeneralization theory
Trust region, proximal methodsTRPO, RL
4.3

AI Applications

  • SGD with momentum: $v \leftarrow \beta v + \nabla L;\; \theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta v$. Still used in all large models.
  • Adam: combines momentum + per-parameter adaptive lr. Default for NLP/CV.
  • AdamW: Adam + decoupled weight decay. Default for LLM training.
  • LR warmup: lr increases linearly for the first N steps then decays. Vital for transformers.
  • Gradient clipping: clip norm to prevent exploding gradients.
  • TRPO/PPO: trust region in RL — don't let the policy change too much per step.
  • DPO: direct preference optimization via implicit reward.
  • GRPO: RLHF without a value network; estimates advantage via group reward. Used by DeepSeek/R1.
  • Muon, SOAP, Shampoo: matrix-valued 2nd-moment optimizers. 2024–2026 results show gains over AdamW on some LLM setups.
4.4

Self-Check

  • Implement SGD, momentum, Adam from scratch.
  • Derive Adam's update rule from definitions.
  • Explain bias correction in Adam: why divide by $(1 - \beta^t)$.
  • Why is decoupled weight decay different from L2 regularization in Adam?
  • Sketch the loss landscape for a convex vs. non-convex problem.
  • Solve an SVM problem using KKT conditions by hand (2D case).
4.5

Common Pitfalls

Warning
  • Assuming Adam always beats SGD — SGD+momentum is still unbeaten for CV in many cases.
  • Forgetting to zero gradients (optimizer.zero_grad()).
  • Setting learning rate too high → loss spike / NaN.
  • Not using warmup when training transformers → loss divergence.
  • Assuming larger batch size = faster training — you need to scale lr with $\sqrt{\text{batch}}$ or linearly.
Soon Needed · Area 5

Information Theory

5.1

Why It Matters

Most classification and generative model losses originate from information theory. Deep understanding of cross-entropy, KL, and MI = fluent reading of modern papers.

TopicWhy You Need It
Entropy $H(X)$Measures uncertainty
Joint, conditional, mutual entropyMeasuring dependence
Cross-entropy $H(p,q)$Classification loss
KL divergence $D_{KL}(p\|q)$VAE, variational inference, distillation
Mutual information $I(X;Y)$Contrastive learning, InfoMax
Jensen-Shannon divergenceGAN
Rate-distortion theoryVAE bottleneck, compression
Channel capacityInformation bottleneck principle
MDL, Kolmogorov complexityOccam's razor theory
f-divergence (generalization)f-GAN, advanced
5.2

AI Applications

  • Cross-entropy loss: every classification loss = $H(p_{\text{true}}, p_{\text{model}})$.
  • KL in VAE: ELBO $= \mathbb{E}[\log p(x|z)] - D_{KL}(q(z|x) \| p(z))$.
  • Knowledge distillation: student learns from teacher via KL on softmax output.
  • InfoNCE (contrastive learning): lower bound of mutual information → SimCLR, CLIP.
  • Perplexity: $\exp(\text{cross-entropy})$ — standard language model metric.
  • β-VAE: scales KL term by β to control disentanglement.
5.3

Common Pitfalls

Warning
  • Forgetting $D_{KL} \geq 0$ and $= 0 \iff p = q$ a.e.
  • Confusing forward KL (mode-covering) with reverse KL (mode-seeking).
  • Assuming MI is easy to estimate — it's extremely hard, requires lower bounds (MINE, InfoNCE).
  • Forgetting log-base: $\log_2$ (bits) vs. $\log_e$ (nats).
Soon Needed · Area 6

Numerical Methods

6.1

Why It Matters

All ML computation runs on finite-precision floats. Understanding floating-point = understanding why loss goes NaN, why FP16 training breaks, why softmax overflows.

TopicWhy You Need It
Floating-point representation (FP32, TF32, FP16, BF16, FP8)Mixed precision training
Round-off errorNumerical stability
Numerical stabilityAvoiding NaN/Inf
Condition numberWhen a matrix is hard to invert
Log-space trick: logsumexpNumerically stable softmax
Catastrophic cancellationError from subtracting near-equal numbers
Iterative methods: Jacobi, Gauss-Seidel, CGSolving large linear systems
Power iteration, LanczosLargest eigenvalue
6.2

AI Applications

  • Stable softmax: $\text{softmax}(x) = \text{softmax}(x - \max(x))$ to prevent overflow.
  • logsumexp: $\log(\sum \exp(x_i)) = \max + \log(\sum \exp(x_i - \max))$.
  • Mixed precision (FP16/BF16/FP8): faster training + less memory. BF16 is default for LLMs.
  • Layer normalization: divides by $\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}$ — ε prevents division by zero.
  • Stable cross-entropy: use logsoftmax + NLLLoss instead of softmax + log + NLL.
6.3

Common Pitfalls

Warning
  • Assuming $0.1 + 0.2 = 0.3$ (it doesn't, due to binary float).
  • Forgetting ε in LayerNorm, BatchNorm, divisions.
  • Training FP16 without loss scaling → gradient underflow.
  • Subtracting two near-equal numbers (catastrophic cancellation) → precision loss.

As Needed · Area 9

Functional Analysis

9.1

Why It Matters

The foundation of kernel methods (SVM, GP). Decreasingly important in deep learning, but still foundational for theory. Key topics: Banach/Hilbert spaces, RKHS, spectral theorem, Fourier analysis.

  • Kernel SVM: maximize margin in RKHS.
  • Gaussian Process: distribution over functions, kernel = covariance.
  • Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK): wide NN ≡ kernel method.
  • Positional encoding: Fourier basis in transformer.
As Needed · Area 10

Differential Geometry & Topology

10.1

Why It Matters

Manifold learning, hyperbolic embeddings, diffusion theory (score = gradient on manifold), loss landscape analysis. Key topics: manifolds, tangent spaces, Riemannian metrics, geodesics, curvature, persistent homology.

  • Manifold hypothesis: real data lies on a low-dimensional manifold in high-dim space.
  • Hyperbolic embedding: for tree/hierarchy data (Poincaré embeddings).
  • Geometric Deep Learning: Bronstein et al. — unifies GNN, CNN, transformer via geometry.
Optional · Area 11

Abstract Algebra

11.1

Why It Matters (Niche)

Group-equivariant networks: AlphaFold, physics ML, computer vision with symmetries. Beautiful in theory, niche in application. Key topics: groups, group actions, representation theory, Lie groups, irreducible representations.

  • Equivariant CNN: group convolution replacing standard convolution.
  • E(3)-equivariant network: AlphaFold, 3D molecular modeling.
  • SE(3)-Transformer: transformer equivariant to rigid transformations.
Optional · Area 12

Measure Theory

12.1

Why It Matters (Very Rare)

Only needed when reading rigorous probability papers (convergence proofs, optimal transport theory). Not needed for practical AI/ML. Key topics: σ-algebra, measures, Lebesgue integral, Radon-Nikodym derivative, dominated convergence, Wasserstein distance.

  • Wasserstein distance: used in WGAN, optimal transport.
  • Radon-Nikodym: the density $p(x)$. Important when changing measures.

Self-Check

  • Derive online softmax (running max + denominator) used in FlashAttention.
  • Implement RoPE by hand on one attention head — verify the rotation is correct.
  • Discretize $\dot{h} = Ah + Bx$ via ZOH, derive the recurrence form of Mamba's selective scan.

13.4

Common Pitfalls

Warning
  • Assuming FlashAttention is "exact" bit-equal — correct in value, different in operation order.
  • Implementing RoPE on the wrong axis → attention becomes noise.
  • Wrong SSM discretization (Euler vs ZOH vs bilinear) → unstable.
  • PTQ quantization looks good on benchmarks but breaks on long-tail inputs.
  • Incorrect speculative decoding → output distribution deviates from target model.
  • Training DPO for only 1 epoch isn't enough; too many epochs → overfitting preference.
Advanced

Applied Math "Beyond the Table"

A

High-ROI Areas After Mastering 1–7

After solidifying areas 1–7, these typically have higher ROI than studying (10)(11)(12):

A. Statistical Learning Theory

PAC learning, VC dimension, Rademacher complexity, generalization bounds. Answers "why do models generalize?". Classical bounds are vacuous for DL; 2020–2026 has PAC-Bayes, NTK-based bounds.

Resource: Shalev-Shwartz & Ben-David — "Understanding Machine Learning".

B. Reinforcement Learning Math

MDP, Bellman equation, policy gradient theorem, TD learning, GAE, importance sampling. Extremely hot in 2026: RLHF, GRPO, reasoning models (o1/R1), agents, robotics.

Resource: Sutton & Barto — "RL: An Introduction" (free PDF). Spinning Up (OpenAI), CleanRL.

C. Advanced Convex Analysis

Subdifferential, proximal operator, ADMM, mirror descent, Bregman divergence. Relevant to optimization research and some LLM optimizers (Muon).

D. Causal Inference

do-calculus, counterfactuals, instrumental variables, DAG, front/back-door criterion. Growing niche, important for rigorous evaluation and LLM debiasing.

Resource: Pearl — "Causality" or "The Book of Why". Cunningham — "Causal Inference: The Mixtape" (free).

E. Game Theory

Nash equilibrium, minimax, mechanism design, no-regret learning. Relevant for GAN, multi-agent RL, alignment, self-play.

F. Computational Complexity

P, NP, approximation, hardness, randomized algorithms. Understand when problems don't scale and why heuristics are needed.

Guidance

Meta-Traps When Studying Math for AI

M

Cross-Cutting Mistakes

Per-area pitfalls are in each section. These are cross-area traps — about how you learn, not what.

Traps
  1. "Learn all the math, then start coding" — you'll never be "ready enough". Learn and build in parallel, triggered by need.
  2. Learning from purely theoretical textbooks — you'll forget immediately. Implementation is mandatory; intuition comes from your fingertips.
  3. Studying advanced areas before mastering fundamentals. Topology before calculus = waste of time. Follow the priority order.
  4. Believing "you need a PhD in math" — wrong. Top engineers at frontier labs learn math on-demand, not systematically from scratch.
  5. Skipping (6) Numerical Methods — you'll spend your life debugging NaN without understanding root cause.
  6. Learning each area in isolation — you'll forget. One project using 3–4 areas simultaneously sticks better than 6 months of reading.
  7. Putting research on a pedestal — self-doubt. Good engineers need math no less than researchers, just along different dimensions.
  8. Not re-evaluating your path every 3 months — sticking to an old path due to sunk cost even when your goals have changed.
  9. "Read 50 papers then reproduce" — reading-without-doing trap. Reproducing 1 paper > reading 20.

R.4

Cross-Path Principles

  • Don't study areas (9)(10)(11)(12) in the first 12 months unless your path explicitly requires it.

Three Principles to Keep
1
Learn by real need. Have a project → need math X → learn math X.
2
Implement, don't just read. Math intuition comes from your fingertips, not your eyes.
3
Pick one path, re-evaluate every 3 months. Path 1 (Engineer) / Path 2 (Research) / Path 3 (Applied) — don't jump due to FOMO.
Focus

If you already have areas (1)(2)(3)(4) at "can use" level and are preparing to enter the LLM era: your priority for the next 6 months should be patching (5) Information Theory + (6) Numerical Methods + (13) LLM-era Math — not racing to learn (10)(11)(12) or measure theory.
